Shafeef Omar

PhD Student @ Applied and Theoretical Aspects of Robot Intelligence (ATARI) Lab. Munich Institute of Robotics and Machine Intelligence (MIRMI). Technical University of Munich (TUM).

Georg-Brauchle-Ring 60-62, 80992 Munich

I am a PhD student with ATARI Lab, supervised by Prof. Majid Khadiv and co-supervised by Prof. Cordelia Schmid.

Previously, I was a research fellow with the Dynamic Legged Systems (DLS) Group, headed by Claudio Semini at the Istituto Italiano di Tecnologia, where I worked on quadrupeds for safe vision-aided locomotion. Before that, I was a Research Associate at the Indian Institute of Science, where I worked on Continual Learning for mitigating catastrophic forgetting in neural networks at the Artificial Intelligence and Robotics Laboratory. I graduated from the Indian Institute of Technology Madras (IIT-M) with an Engineering Dual Degree, Bachelor and Master of Technology.

My research focuses on generalizable robot learning, especially humanoid loco-manipulation: coordinating locomotion, balance, and interaction with objects. I combine reinforcement learning, trajectory optimization, and diffusion models to learn diverse behaviors from limited demonstrations and autonomous motion discovery. I am also interested in motion retargeting, safe adaptation on hardware, and vision-language-action models (VLAs).

Recent work includes DiPLO, which combines a self-distilled diffusion planner with a whole-body tracker; MotionDisco, which discovers humanoid skills without demonstrations; and DynaRetarget, which turns human motions into dynamically feasible robot trajectories. See Google Scholar for my full publication list.

2026

  1. DiPLO: Self-distilled Diffusion Planning for Loco-manipulation
    Shafeef Omar, Sebastian Sanokowski, Elham Daneshmand, Dian Yu, Daniel Cremers, Cordelia Schmid, and Majid Khadiv
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2610.24847, 2026
    Abs arXiv Bib PDF Website Video

    DiPLO pairs a diffusion planner with an RL-trained whole-body tracker for humanoid loco-manipulation. Self-distillation removes the planner’s dependence on reference-motion conditioning, and reinforcement learning then improves its plans with the tracker held fixed. The resulting system plans from state history and object goals, generalizes to new goals, and executes pick-carry-place motions on a Unitree G1 without real-world fine-tuning.

  2. MotionDisco: Motion Discovery for Extreme Humanoid Loco-Manipulation
    Ilyass Taouil*, Michal Ciebielski*, Shafeef Omar*, Haizhou Zhao, Angela Dai, Aaron M. Johnson, and Majid Khadiv
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2606.06139, 2026
    Equal contribution: Ilyass Taouil, Michal Ciebielski, and Shafeef Omar.
    Abs arXiv Bib PDF Website Video

    MotionDisco discovers humanoid loco-manipulation motions without demonstrations or teleoperation. Language-model-guided evolutionary search proposes contact interactions, while a trajectory optimizer checks and refines the resulting whole-body motions. RL tracking policies transfer the discovered skills to a real humanoid robot.

  3. DynaRetarget: Dynamically-Feasible Retargeting using Sampling-Based Trajectory Optimization
    Victor Dhédin*, Ilyass Taouil*, Shafeef Omar*, Dian Yu, Kun Tao, Angela Dai, and Majid Khadiv
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2602.06827, 2026
    Equal contribution: Victor Dhédin, Ilyass Taouil, and Shafeef Omar.
    Abs arXiv Bib PDF Code Website Video

    DynaRetarget converts human demonstrations into dynamically feasible humanoid motions. Sampling-based trajectory optimization refines kinematic retargeting results over progressively longer horizons, handling variations in object mass, size, and geometry. The resulting motions support training humanoid control policies and generating synthetic loco-manipulation data.

  4. Learning to Act Through Contact: A Unified View of Multi-Task Robot Learning
    Shafeef Omar*, and Majid Khadiv
    In Proceedings of The 8th Annual Learning for Dynamics and Control Conference, 2026
    Abs arXiv Bib HTML PDF Video

    This work represents locomotion and manipulation tasks as sequences of contact goals: where and when contact should occur, and which end-effectors should be active. A goal-conditioned RL policy learns multiple skills within each robotic setting, including quadrupedal gaits, humanoid locomotion, and bimanual object manipulation. Explicit contact goals improve generalization to unseen tasks.

  1. SafeSteps: Learning Safer Footstep Planning Policies for Legged Robots via Model-Based Priors
    Shafeef Omar*, Lorenzo Amatucci, Victor Barasuol, Giulio Turrisi, and Claudio Semini
    In IEEE-RAS International Conference on Humanoid Robots, 2023
    Abs arXiv Bib PDF Website

    We present a footstep planning policy for quadrupedal locomotion that is able to directly take into consideration a-priori safety information in its decisions. At its core, a learning process analyzes terrain patches, classifying each landing location by its kinematic feasibility, shin collision, and terrain roughness. This information is then encoded into a small vector representation and passed as an additional state to the footstep planning policy, which furthermore proposes only safe footstep location by applying a masked variant of the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm [1]. The performance of the proposed approach is shown by comparative simulations on an electric quadruped robot walking in different rough terrain scenarios. We show that violations of the above safety conditions are greatly reduced both during training and the successive deployment of the policy, resulting in an inherently safer footstep planner. Furthermore, we show how, as a byproduct, fewer reward terms are needed to shape the behavior of the policy, which in return is able to achieve both better final performances and sample efficiency.
